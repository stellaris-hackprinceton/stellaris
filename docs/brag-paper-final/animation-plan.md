## Direction

The app workflow leads the film. Each transition should express a change in the work: asking, finding, designing, running, collecting, measuring, interpreting. Keep the actual app capture readable and make the framing, captions, and connecting transitions do most of the animation. Do not add a Codex-making-of segment or on-screen discussion of how the promotional video was produced.

Use a common motion language across distinct section treatments: smooth acceleration, short travel, decisive settling, no rubbery bounce. Alternate energetic transitions with stable working scenes. Do not move the camera throughout every shot.

Selected: **precise motion with a few spatial transitions** (the proposed default). A more restrained alternative replaces the folding and depth moves below with flat masked slides. A more expressive alternative increases depth only in the hook, model-to-physical transition, and close. The actual recordings and figures remain undistorted in every variant.

## Section entrances and exits

### 1. Paper hook · 0–4 s

Entrance: reveal the three portrait stills through upward-moving masks, staggered by 4 frames. Each mask opens in 12 frames. Shape labels fade in together once the panels settle. The headline rises 16 px over 12 frames.

Exit: panels move outward a short distance and fade over 12 frames, leaving the center clear for the next statement. Their images remain flat. Do not fold or bend scientific footage.

Beat opportunity: three quiet subdivisions for panel arrivals, one stronger accent when the headline settles. Avoid three large impact sounds.

### 2. Scattered tools → connected work · 4–10 s

Entrance: small editorial tool labels appear at separated positions on alternating beats. No continuous drifting once the statement needs to be read.

Exit: labels follow short curved paths toward a central project frame over 18 frames. The headline dissolves as the last label arrives. This is an explicitly editorial diagram.

Beat opportunity: the final convergence lands on a downbeat. This is one of the film's largest animation moments.

### 3. Brand → app · 10–13 s

Entrance: the mark resolves from that center with an opacity reveal and 96%→100% scale over 15 frames. The wordmark reveals through a horizontal mask.

Exit: the outer project frame expands into the real screen-capture bounds over 18 frames. The mark fades away rather than being presented as a nonexistent app splash screen.

Beat opportunity: a single soft musical accent at the mark, then a small pickup into the app.

### 4. Question and chat · 13–25 s

Entrance: the real app capture enters as one stable plane. A subtle editorial outline draws around the actual composer in 10 frames. Genuine typing begins once the view settles.

Exit: the outline retracts over 8 frames. A short horizontal masked slide introduces the sources excerpt. Send→response latency is a direct cut, not a flashy transition.

Beat opportunity: Send can align to an accent by shifting the capture's editorial start. Do not retime individual keystrokes to the music or require the real agent to finish on a beat.

### 5. Sources · 25–36 s

Exit: an underline beneath the selected evidence extends toward the next scene, then dissolves as the protocol view wipes in over 12 frames. Do not animate invented source cards as if returned by the app.

### 6. Experiment instructions · 36–46 s

Exit: a thin selection frame closes around the actual model/code area in the next capture, with a 12-frame position transition. Do not collapse the protocol before its copy has been read.

### 7. Simulation · 46–59 s

Figure entrance: reveal the saved simulation figure with a left-to-right mask over 18 frames. The mask exposes the existing whole figure, it does not manufacture an evolving calculation or redraw data values.

### 11. Interpretation · 93–107 s

Entrance: a margin rule draws beside the actual report passage over 12 frames. A translucent editorial emphasis behind the supported finding reveals over 10 frames without obscuring text.

Mid-scene: emphasis crossfades from finding to limitation over 10 frames. Hold both excerpts separately long enough to read. No word-by-word bouncing text.

Exit: emphasis fades and the report frame shifts backward by a shallow 2% scale over 15 frames to make room for the technical context.

Beat opportunity: a calmer musical phrase supports reading. One gentle accent for finding → limitation, no celebratory success sound or claim before evidence review.

### 12. Technical context · 107–114 s

Beat opportunity: one shared entrance on the phrase downbeat, leaving most of the section still.

### 13. Close · 114–118 s

Entrance: the actual project view gently recedes and fades over 18 frames. Mark and tagline reveal through a clean centered mask. Optional subtle violet light expansion once, with no persistent pulse.

Exit: hold the settled identity at least two seconds. Fade audio through the final two seconds. Keep a deliberate final image rather than fading all content to black before the end.

Beat opportunity: the logo arrives on the closing musical resolution. Music must resolve naturally within the 118-second cut.

## Music direction to audition

Primary candidate style: **instrumental downtempo electronica with a precise pulse**, warm bass, restrained drums, a repeating synth motif, and a slight sense of curiosity. Target roughly **112–120 BPM with a relaxed feel**, clear beats, and audible 4/8-bar phrases. Prefer a track with an introduction, a modest build around execution/physical testing, a quieter interpretation passage, and a clean ending. A tasteful breakbeat or minimal electronic groove is welcome if it leaves room for typing and reading.

Alternative styles for selection:

- **Minimal electronic:** fewer drums, crisp pulse, spacious synths. Suits the more restrained motion variant.
- **Textural breakbeat:** soft syncopated percussion and warm melodic detail. Suits more expressive spatial transitions without becoming a trailer.

Exclude generic corporate/business music, ukulele, stock motivational piano, claps-and-whistles, bombastic trailer drops, vocals, and happy-business bundled tracks. No track has been chosen or audited yet. Audition actual excerpts and verify license/attribution before final selection. A track's genre or BPM alone does not establish that it will work.

After selection, detect actual beat and phrase timestamps. Lock major editorial entrances to phrase accents, with smaller caption cues on selected subdivisions. Keep chapter boundaries at the existing seconds unless a later approved timing revision improves the cut. Use an appropriate track start/edit point or adjust motion accents within a section. Do not claim the current integer-second scene starts already coincide with a musical grid.

## Sound treatment

- **Typing:** soft, low-frequency keyboard taps with varied samples and small pauses. Reduce sharp high-end, use short envelopes, and avoid a per-character machine-gun effect. Align sounds to visible genuine typing. No sound under agent response text. A paste receives one subdued cue.
- **Paper:** added paper texture is optional and low in the mix. Fade each added accent in over roughly 80–120 ms and out over 250–400 ms. No abrupt rustle or exaggerated impact. Natural source audio may be blended gently, with short crossfades between clips, or omitted when distracting. Do not fade or alter preserved evidence originals.
- **Music:** carries the rhythm. Typing sits lightly above it while paper is background texture. Drop the earlier rule requiring music 8–12 dB below paper/typing. Judge the new mix by listening, then meet the delivery loudness/peak targets.
- **Transitions:** most motion needs no added whoosh. A few subdued tonal or air cues may support the tool convergence, model→physical move, and closing resolution. Avoid a sound effect on every section change.
